Uber and Lyft Accident Lawyer Los Angeles

A rideshare crash in Los Angeles is not a normal car-accident claim. Three different insurance periods apply—based on whether the driver’s app was off, on but waiting, or actively carrying a passenger—and the available coverage can jump from a personal auto policy to a $1 million commercial policy depending on the period. This guide explains the three periods, what evidence proves which one applied, and how to request a free claim review.

The Three Rideshare Insurance Periods

California law breaks every Uber or Lyft trip into three periods. The available insurance is very different in each one, which is why the first question after any rideshare crash is what was the driver doing the second the crash happened?

  • Period 0 (app off). The driver is not logged in. Only the driver’s personal auto policy is in play. Uber and Lyft’s commercial coverage does not apply.
  • Period 1 (app on, no ride accepted). The driver is online and waiting for a ride request. Uber and Lyft are required to maintain at least $50,000 / $100,000 / $30,000 in third-party liability coverage, plus the driver’s personal policy in most cases.
  • Period 2 (en route to passenger) and Period 3 (passenger on board). Uber and Lyft each maintain a $1 million third-party liability policy plus $1 million in uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage. This is what most people think of when they hear “Uber has a $1M policy.”

Which period applied is rarely a guess—it can be proved from the app log, the rider’s receipt, and platform GPS data. Preserving those records early matters.

Common Rideshare Crash Scenarios

  • You were a passenger and the Uber or Lyft driver caused the crash, or another vehicle hit your rideshare.
  • You were a driver of another vehicle hit by an Uber or Lyft driver who was on a trip.
  • You were a pedestrian or cyclist hit by a rideshare driver.
  • You were the rideshare driver and another vehicle was at fault.

Passengers usually have the cleanest claim because they have no responsibility for the crash. The hard work is figuring out the right insurer and not being pushed into an early settlement before injuries are fully evaluated.

Evidence That Decides These Claims

  • The trip receipt, ride history, and in-app messages on the passenger’s phone
  • The platform’s ride status and GPS logs (preserve via a written request to Uber or Lyft)
  • Dashcam, rideshare-cam, or nearby surveillance footage
  • Police report and witness statements
  • Photographs of all involved vehicles, lane positions, and scene markings
  • Medical records that document injury onset within hours, not weeks, of the crash

Why Insurance Disputes Happen

Uber and Lyft have argued in past cases that drivers were “off the app” or in a transition between periods, which can shift the responsible insurer from the $1 million commercial policy to a personal auto policy with much lower limits. The other side’s insurer may try to delay until the app data is harder to retrieve. Documenting period status quickly is the single biggest lever in a rideshare claim.

Compensation Under California Law

  • Medical treatment, including future care
  • Lost wages and reduced earning capacity
  • Pain, suffering, and emotional distress
  • Out-of-pocket losses (transportation, household help, medications)
  • Property damage to a personal vehicle if you were not the rideshare passenger

FAQs

Which insurance policy applies in an Uber or Lyft crash?

It depends on the period: a personal policy in Period 0, a smaller commercial policy in Period 1, and a $1 million commercial policy in Periods 2 and 3. The app log and trip receipt usually decide the question.

What if the rideshare driver was off-app?

Then only the driver’s personal auto policy is in play. Uber and Lyft’s commercial coverage does not apply when the app is not on.

Can passengers recover compensation?

Yes. Passengers have no fault for the crash and can pursue claims against the at-fault driver’s insurance, the rideshare company’s commercial policy when it applies, and uninsured-motorist coverage if the at-fault driver fled or had no insurance.

Do I have to use Uber or Lyft’s claim portal?

You can usually contact the carrier directly. Before signing any release or giving a recorded statement, it is reasonable to get a second opinion.

Was the driver an employee or an independent contractor?

Uber and Lyft classify drivers as independent contractors. Under California Proposition 22 and the rideshare-specific insurance rules, the commercial-coverage requirements still apply during Periods 1-3 regardless of classification.
